Google Research Enhances Federated Learning with TEEs and External Verifiability

Google Research has introduced a new federated learning system that moves gradient computation into attested server-side Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs), bolstering privacy. This innovation allows external verification of differential privacy, with Gboard already implementing it for next-word prediction.

Why this is happening
  • Growing demand for privacy-preserving AI training methods.
  • Federated learning enables models to learn from decentralized data.
  • Trusted Execution Environments provide hardware-level security.
  • Transparency and external verification build user trust in AI systems.
Possible knock-on effects
  • Could set a new standard for privacy and verifiability in federated learning.
  • May accelerate adoption of TEEs in privacy-sensitive AI applications.
  • Likely to boost confidence in AI systems handling personal data.

Aleph Alpha Launches Kolibri, a 78.1B Parameter English-German MoE Model

Aleph Alpha has released Kolibri, a new 78.1B-parameter English-German Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model, notable for activating only 3.46B parameters per token. With a 1M-token context and Apache 2.0 FP8 weights, it offers efficient performance on single H200 or B200 GPUs.

Why this is happening
  • Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models offer efficiency for large parameter counts.
  • Demand for performant and resource-efficient multilingual AI.
  • Open-weight models foster broader research and development.
  • Hardware limitations often dictate model practicality.
Possible knock-on effects
  • Could lower the barrier to entry for deploying large-scale multilingual AI.
  • May increase adoption of MoE architectures in commercial applications.
  • Likely to spur competition in developing resource-efficient frontier models.
